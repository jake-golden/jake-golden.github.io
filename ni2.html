Precision Impedance, at the Spring-Pin Plane

Verification, validation, and reference-design engineering for the PXIe-4190 LCR meter/SMU — NI's precision impedance module for semiconductor test systems.

At sub-picofarad and picoampere sensitivity up to 2 MHz, the instrument is not the limiting factor — the cable and the customer's board are. I authored and executed a system-level V&V test plan measuring performance where the DUT actually sits, and designed a Device Interface Board that turns qualitative layout guidance into characterized numbers.

The FET Guard

A single switch guards the DUT-side node but leaves the switch package itself unguarded. Splitting each conductor across two series PhotoMOS switches and clamping the intermediate node to guard potential terminates the package parasitics into the buffer instead of the measurement.

The switch is in the guarded path, but nothing guards the switch.

Packages leak into the buffer, not the measurement.

04 / PCB Engineering

One Stackup, Three Cross-Sections

Conventional PCB design references everything to ground. This board inverts that: the plane adjacent to each sensitive signal is a driven guard.

Three cross-sections were needed, but adding layers wasn't an option. Solved with per-region, per-channel net reassignment — the same six physical layers take different net roles in different board regions.

Why 2 MHz Turns Cables Into Transmission Lines

Click to expand

At DC a cable is a wire. At 2 MHz it is a distributed network whose length changes the impedance the instrument thinks it sees.

Cable model: lumped vs. distributed

Equivalent circuits with the error term called out

  • Cable C and L stop behaving as single lumped elements
