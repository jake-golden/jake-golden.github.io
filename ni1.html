Measuring the Thing That Measures Everything

The PXIe-4190 is NI's precision impedance module for semiconductor test systems — resolving sub-picofarad capacitance and picoampere currents at frequencies up to 2 MHz.

At that sensitivity, the instrument is rarely the limiting factor. The cable and the customer's board are. Parasitics that are utterly irrelevant in ordinary PCB design become the dominant error source.

My work had two halves: authoring and executing a full system-level V&V test plan that characterizes performance at the spring-pin plane rather than the instrument's front panel, and designing a custom Device Interface Board (DIB) to quantify exactly how customer layout choices degrade that accuracy — turning "avoid X" into "X costs you Y ppm."

01 / Foundation

Measurement Theory, From First Principles

Guarding

A guard is a conductor actively driven to track the signal's own potential. If the guard sits at the same voltage as the signal it surrounds, the stray capacitance between them sees zero ΔV — and a capacitor with no voltage across it draws no current.

The parasitic still physically exists. It simply stops mattering, because the leakage current it would have stolen from the measurement is instead supplied by the guard buffer.

Every stray capacitance to ground sees the full signal swing and bleeds current the meter counts as DUT admittance.

The guard absorbs the leakage. The signal path only ever sees the device under test.

4-Terminal-Pair (4TP) Kelvin Connection

Force and sense are separated so that lead resistance and contact resistance never appear in the measured value.

The Auto-Balancing Bridge

Click to expand

The measurement architecture behind the instrument: a null detector holds the low terminal at virtual ground while an I-V converter measures exactly the current the DUT sources.

Auto-balancing bridge block diagram

Null detector, range resistor, I-V converter, vector ratio detector

  • Low terminal held at virtual ground by feedback, not by a physical short
  • Impedance derived from the complex ratio of applied voltage to measured current
  • Balance quality degrades with frequency — the reason 2 MHz is the ceiling
Why 2 MHz Turns Cables Into Transmission Lines

At DC a cable is a wire. At 2 MHz it is a distributed network whose length changes the impedance the instrument thinks it is looking at.

02 / Verification & Validation

A Test Plan That Knows Its Own Order

I authored a 15+ section formal test plan covering the entire cable and DIB signal chain — connectivity, isolation, capacitance, LCR accuracy, compensation and its stability, repeatability across docking cycles, and the effect of the instrument's own timing settings.

The part that took real judgment wasn't the tests — it was the sequencing. Several tests silently depend on earlier ones passing, and the original document numbering had ordering dependencies backwards. I restructured execution into four gated phases.

Why the Ordering Actually Matters

Click to expand

A test plan executed in the wrong order produces numbers that look valid and mean nothing.

  • Compensation before connectivity bakes an intermittent contact into the correction coefficients, and every later measurement inherits it.
  • Accuracy before compensation characterizes the fixture, not the instrument.
  • DC bias before settling time reads incomplete settling as a real bias coefficient — a physically plausible wrong answer, which is the dangerous kind.
  • Phase gating makes each stage's validity a precondition for starting the next.

03 / Reference Design

Quantifying the Customer's Own Mistakes

The DIB exists to answer one question: how much accuracy does each common board-design choice actually cost? Four error sources, each isolated on its own characterization channel so the effect can be measured rather than asserted.

The FET Guard

A single switch in the signal path guards the DUT-side node but leaves the switch package itself unguarded — its own off-state leakage and package capacitance land directly on the measurement.

The fix: split each conductor across two series PhotoMOS switches and actively clamp the intermediate node to guard potential. The package parasitics now terminate into the guard buffer instead of the signal.

The switch is in the guarded path, but nothing guards the switch.

Two switches in series with the midpoint driven to guard — the packages leak into the buffer, not the measurement.

04 / PCB Engineering

One Stackup, Three Cross-Sections

Conventional PCB design references everything to ground. This board inverts that assumption: the reference plane adjacent to each sensitive signal is a driven guard, not ground.

The hard part was the layer budget. Three distinct cross-sections were needed — but adding layers for each was not an option.

Solved with per-region, per-channel net reassignment: the same six physical layers take different net roles in different board regions. Three cross-sections, one stackup, zero added layers.
